# 📜 Agile Team Charter

## 1. Purpose & Vision
**Vision:** To bridge the digital divide in Khayelitsha by providing affordable, reliable, uncapped home internet to residents and small business owners.
**Value Proposition:** Ekhaya Connect provides a competitively priced fixed wireless service (R399/month) so that residents can access education, job opportunities and entertainment without exceeding their household budgets.

## 2. Scope
**In Scope:**
*   Procuring a high-speed fibre link.
*   Installing small wireless dishes at customer homes.
*   Providing a 10 Mbps uncapped internet package.
**Out of Scope:**
*   Building physical fibre-to-the-home (FTTH) infrastructure.
*   Providing hardware (laptops/phones) to residents.

## 3. Working Agreements (Team Values)
*   **Communication:** Daily standups via WhatsApp/Trello.
*   **Respect:** All ideas are valid during brainstorming.
*   **Accountability:** Each member completes their assigned Trello cards.
*   **Transparency:** Blockers must be raised immediately.

## 5. Problem Validation & Evidence
We conducted informal interviews with 17 households in Khayelitsha.
*   **Total Interviews:** 17 households
*   **Willingness to pay:** 14 out of 17 (82%)
*   **High frustration levels:** 14 out of 17 (82%)
*   **Key Insight:** Many households pay R409 to R799 for satellite TV and mobile data. They are highly willing to switch to a R399 uncapped internet service.

## 6. Reflection
Creating the Agile Team Charter helped me realize that a project is not just about the final product, but about setting clear boundaries and shared values from day one. By defining what was "out of scope" (like building physical fibre), we prevented our team from getting overwhelmed. My role involved contributing to the primary research and helping set up our working agreements. I also learned the importance of validation; interviewing 17 households gave us the confidence to proceed. It taught me that a charter is a living document that grounds the team in the reality of the community's needs.
